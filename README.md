# Healthcare Insurance Charges: Exploratory Data Analysis & Statistical Analysis

## Project Overview

This project analyzes a medical insurance dataset to examine patterns in insurance charges and explore relationships between charges and selected demographic and health-related variables.

The analysis was conducted in Microsoft Excel using descriptive statistics, grouped comparisons, correlation analysis, data visualization, and multiple linear regression.

## Objective

The goal of this analysis was to investigate how insurance charges vary across different characteristics and determine whether selected variables provide useful information for understanding differences in medical insurance charges.

## Research Questions

1. How do medical insurance charges vary by age?
2. How does BMI relate to medical insurance charges?
3. Are insurance charges different between smokers and non-smokers?
4. How do insurance charges vary by number of children?
5. Does age, BMI, smoking status, or number of children have the strongest relationship with insurance charges?
6. Can age and BMI be used to predict insurance charges?

## Tools & Methods

* Microsoft Excel
* Descriptive statistics
* Grouped analysis
* Correlation analysis
* Data visualization
* Multiple linear regression

## Key Findings

* Average insurance charges increased across each age group, from approximately $9,182 for individuals ages 18–29 to approximately $21,248 for individuals age 60+.
* BMI had a weak positive relationship with insurance charges (r = 0.1983).
* Average insurance charges were substantially higher for smokers ($32,050) than for non-smokers ($8,434) in this dataset.
* Average charges varied by number of children, although groups with 4 and 5 children contained relatively few records.
* Age had the strongest correlation with charges among the numerical variables examined (r = 0.2990).
* A regression model using age and BMI explained 11.8% of the variation in insurance charges, indicating that other factors contribute substantially to differences in charges.

## Statistical Analysis

A multiple linear regression model was used to examine whether age and BMI could predict insurance charges.

The resulting model explained 11.8% of the variation in charges. Age and BMI were statistically significant predictors within the model, but the relatively low R² indicates that these variables alone provide limited explanatory power for individual insurance charges.

## Conclusion

The analysis identified clear differences in insurance charges across age groups and smoking status, while BMI showed a weaker relationship with charges. The regression analysis demonstrated that age and BMI provide some predictive information but are insufficient on their own to explain most of the variation in insurance charges.

This project demonstrates the use of Excel for exploratory data analysis, statistical analysis, visualization, and interpretation of real-world data.
